Interrogatory & Motion Drafter

Drafts plaintiff's answers to Form A uniform interrogatories, answers to the defense's supplemental questions, and routine motion papers from the facts of a matter, entered once. Every answer is checked by the paralegal and approved by the attorney before it is served.

This is a demonstration. Anything entered stays in the browser on the computer being used.

How it works

The same steps appear, in order, down the left side of each demo.

  1. Enter the facts. Fill in the first five screens from the police report, the medical records, the client, and the attorney's decisions. Anything an answer still needs is outlined in red and labeled with the question that needs it.
  2. Review the Form A answers. Each answer is drafted from those screens. Where an item is missing, choose Fill in to go straight to the right box, then go back to the answer. The wording can be edited, and standard language or an objection can be inserted.
  3. Sign off. The paralegal marks each answer as checked, and the attorney then approves it. If the wording changes after either sign-off, the answer is flagged to be checked again.
  4. Supplemental questions. Paste the defense's questions as served. The firm's standard answers are suggested where they fit. Questions past the tenth, or with subparts, are flagged with the matching objection under R. 4:17-1(b)(1).
  5. Finish and serve. See the deadlines, the attachment and HIPAA authorization checklists, and the finished answers, ready to download as a Word file or print.

In the blank version, choose Save before leaving a screen. The example saves as you type.

Other tools

  • Motion papers. Choose the motion and fill in a few details. The notice, certification, proposed order, and filing deadlines are prepared from the matter. Places that call for the attorney's argument are marked.
  • Client questionnaire. A plain-language questionnaire for the client, sent blank or filled in for the client to confirm.
  • Forms library and Firm settings. Change question wording, standard answers, objections, and letterhead. Changes apply to every matter.
